# Puberty: What Happens and When

## What is puberty?

Puberty is the time when your body changes from a child's body into an adult's body. It happens because of hormones — chemical messengers your body starts making in larger amounts.

Puberty is normal. Everyone goes through it. It does not happen on the same schedule for everyone, and that is also normal.

## When does puberty start?

For most girls, puberty begins between ages 8 and 13. For most boys, it begins between ages 9 and 14.

Starting earlier or later than your friends does not mean anything is wrong. Bodies follow their own timing. If you started much earlier than 8 or have not started by about 14, it is worth talking to a health worker — not because something is wrong, but so someone can check.

## Body changes in girls

These usually happen in roughly this order, over several years:

- **Breast buds develop.** Small, sometimes tender lumps under the nipples. One side often starts before the other. This is usually the first sign.
- **Height increases quickly.** A growth spurt. You may grow several centimetres in a year.
- **Pubic and underarm hair grows.**
- **Hips widen and body shape changes.** You gain some fat around the hips, thighs and chest. This is a healthy, expected part of development.
- **Periods begin.** Usually about 2 to 3 years after breast buds first appear.
- **Skin becomes oilier.** Pimples and acne are common.
- **Sweat changes.** You sweat more and body odour becomes stronger.
- **Vaginal discharge starts.** A clear or whitish fluid. This is normal and often begins several months before the first period.

## Body changes in boys

- Testicles and penis grow larger
- Pubic, underarm and facial hair grows
- Voice deepens, sometimes cracking along the way
- Shoulders broaden and muscle develops
- Growth spurt, usually later than girls
- Skin becomes oilier; acne is common
- Erections and ejaculation begin, including during sleep ("wet dreams")

## Feelings change too

Puberty affects your mind and emotions, not only your body:

- Moods may shift quickly, sometimes for no clear reason
- You may feel more self-conscious about how you look
- You may start feeling attraction to other people
- You may want more privacy and independence from family
- You may feel more stress about friendships and belonging

All of this is a normal part of growing up. Strong feelings are not a sign that something is wrong with you.

## Everyone develops differently

There is no "correct" body. Breasts come in different sizes and shapes. One breast is often slightly larger than the other. Height, weight, hair and skin all vary widely between people.

Comparing yourself to friends, or to images online, is a common source of worry — and those images are often edited. Your body is not late, wrong, or failing because it looks different from someone else's.

## Taking care of your changing body

- **Wash daily**, including the underarms and genital area, with clean water. Plain soap on the outside is fine.
- **The vagina cleans itself.** Do not put soap, herbs, or any other substance inside it. This can cause infection.
- **Change underwear daily.** Cotton underwear is more comfortable and healthier than synthetic.
- **Wash your face** with clean water twice a day if you have acne. Avoid squeezing pimples, which can cause scarring.
- **Eat well and drink water.** Your body is growing quickly and needs iron-rich foods especially, such as beans, green leafy vegetables, liver and eggs.
- **Sleep.** Adolescents need roughly 8 to 10 hours a night.

## When to talk to a health worker

Speak to a nurse, clinical officer or doctor if:

- You have not had any signs of puberty by age 14
- You started developing before age 8
- A breast lump is hard, growing, or painful in a way that does not come and go
- You feel persistently sad, hopeless or anxious for more than two weeks
