01 Use case corpus, audience, success metrics

A young woman asks a question she can't ask anyone else.

Young women in Kenya — many in rural areas and informal settlements — face pregnancy, contraception and their periods with little reliable information and few people to ask. The gap is filled by myths, and by clinic visits that come too late. This assistant answers only from a curated corpus, in a register a shy 19-year-old trusts.

Audience
  • Young women aged 18–26, Kenya — rural areas and informal settlements
  • Including expectant mothers in their prenatal period
  • Often shy, or asking "for a friend"
  • Carrying myths from home or online
  • Often with no one they can ask
Corpus
  • Periods: the cycle, pain, management, red flags
  • Family planning and emergency contraception
  • Pregnancy, antenatal care and the weeks after birth
  • Sexual health, consent and care after violence
  • Myths and the questions young women ask
  • 5,137 words · closed · cited
Success metrics
  • Grounded in corpus, not invented
  • Refuses cleanly when out of scope
  • Always routes disclosure of harm
  • Readable at grade 6 — six years of schooling

02 Q&A flow what happens when she asks

The server mints a key, then stays off the media path.

One HTTPS request, then one long-lived socket. The platform signs a URL over HTTPS; everything after that — speech in, transcription, retrieval, the reply, audio out — streams over a single WebSocket held open for the conversation, because two-way audio cannot be done with request and response. The connection upgrades to WebRTC where the browser supports it. Staying off the media path is a design choice, not an absence of one — it keeps a hop out of every turn and leaves no second copy of the conversation. The platform’s work sits around that path: authentication and origin control, the corpus and configuration, analytics, and the sandboxed execution runtime. On voice, SMS and WhatsApp the platform is the endpoint — every turn passes through it.

03 Architecture frameworks and where state lives

What runs where, and what the platform never stores.

Admin console

Corpus upload and the conversation dashboard both live there — changing what Bonga knows, or reading what she said today, is a programme task rather than an engineering one.

It is what would let a health educator correct a myth in the corpus, re-upload the document, then read the conversations that follow to see whether the question keeps coming back — without a redeploy or a developer. Transcripts are read live from the conversations API, not from a copy.

bonga-nami.onrender.com

Orchestration runtime

Bonga answers questions. The same platform can also act on them — and that path already exists, at /webhooks/execute.

When a request needs more than an answer, the model doesn't call tools one at a time. It writes a short JavaScript program, and the runtime executes it against bound functions that reach the organisation's own systems, returning a single result.

Why that matters for tokens: every separate tool call is another round trip, and every round trip re-sends the entire conversation context to the model. Five tool calls means five times paying for the same context. One generated program collapses them into a single execution — fewer tokens, lower latency, and fewer places for the conversation to stall while she waits.

04 LLM & prompt engineering decisions and why

Each setting, and the reasoning behind it.

Generation
gemini-2.5-flash
Voice demands sub-second turns. A larger model buys reasoning this task doesn't need — the answer is in a 5,137-word corpus, not in the model's cleverness.
Temperature
0
Health answers shouldn't vary run to run, and the evaluation must be reproducible. Warmth comes from the prompt, not from sampling noise.
Prompt structure
8 sections
Personality, Environment, Tone, Language, Goal, Boundaries, Safety, Certainty. Markdown headings are prioritised more reliably than prose paragraphs.
Persona
young mother, as a friend
A few steps ahead on the same road — not a clinician, not a lecturer. The register that gets a shy 19-year-old, or a first-time expectant mother, to ask a second question.
Corpus binding
explicit
Instructed to answer only from the knowledge base and to say so when it can't — then enforced independently at the platform layer, because a prompt alone can be talked around.
Anatomy language
correct terms
Euphemism teaches shame and makes disclosure of abuse harder. Explicitly instructed against slang for body parts.

05 Guardrails the audience-specific work

Using the standard content filters would end the conversation at the moment it matters most.

sexual · violence · harassment · self_harm
profanity · religion_or_politics
medical_and_legal_information

one action, shared by all seven ↓
trigger_action : end_call

A user types "sometimes I think about hurting myself." The classifier works exactly as designed — and the call ends.

A filter scans content, not direction. It cannot tell an agent encouraging self-harm from a user disclosing it.

No medical diagnoses    retry
No medication dosing    retry
Safeguarding must route  retry
Must stay grounded      retry

So the filters are off, and four rules regenerate a bad answer instead.

The safeguarding rule is the inverse of a filter: it doesn't suppress the topic, it requires reassurance, someone she trusts and the helpline.

10/10 safeguarding recall, in both evaluation runs — including the one where retrieval was broken, because this path never touches the corpus. But neither setting is the right answer: a disclosure should reach a human counsellor, not a hang-up. transfer_to_number already exists on the agent — disabled, because a PoC has no counsellor line to dial.

06 Evaluation approach what is measured, and how

Measuring accuracy alone would score this system as working while it fails.

Accuracy — any RAG system measures these
  • Groundedness ≥ 95%
  • Refusal accuracy ≥ 90%

Is every claim traceable to the corpus? And when the corpus doesn't cover something, does it say so?

This audience — generic evals omit all three
  • Safeguarding recall = 100%
  • Reading level ≤ grade 6
  • Myth correction ≥ 90%

A disclosure of abuse answered as though it were a question about cramps is a failure no accuracy metric registers. 100%, not 95% — at 95%, one woman in twenty gets nothing.

A true answer that was still a hallucination

"A tampon can't get lost — your vagina is a closed canal." Correct, reassuring, and nowhere in the corpus.

All three judges flagged it. It matters because it is true — a reviewer skimming for wrong answers would approve it.

Two identical situations, opposite behaviour

The corpus-binding rule holds when the model is unsure and lapses when it "knows".

MY07 said "my knowledge base doesn't have that". MY14 answered from memory. The grounding guardrail should have caught it.

Both groundedness misses trace to the same cause: corpus binding is currently a prompt instruction, and a prompt can be talked around by a model that is confident. Enforcing it at the platform layer, where the guardrails already run, would address both. The reading-level miss is sentence length, not vocabulary — the answers use simpler words than the corpus itself, in sentences about four words longer. That fix is not applied: shortening sentences pressures the model to compress figures, which is the same numeric drift the groundedness metric exists to catch.

08 Trade-offs limitations and alternatives considered

What this costs.

Evaluated offline, not with users

Quality is measured against a golden set scored by three independent judges — repeatable, cheap, and runnable on every change.

The cost: no client has used it yet. A judge can score groundedness and refusal; it cannot tell me whether a young woman felt safe enough to ask the second question. That answer only comes from moderated sessions with the real audience, and it would change the prompt before it changed the code.

Corpus scope

Sexual and reproductive health: periods, family planning, pregnancy and antenatal care, the weeks after birth, and sexual health and safety.

The cost: a larger corpus to keep accurate and clinically reviewed, and the evaluation above covers the first three parts. The gain: the questions young women and expectant mothers actually bring — contraception, pregnancy, consent — are answered rather than refused.

English corpus, Swahili replies

A question asked in Swahili is answered in Swahili, from the same English corpus. The corpus and the golden set are English; the conversation is not.

The cost: the 85 cases run in English, so the metrics above describe English answers. Swahili is covered by regression tests rather than measured at that resolution — translating the corpus and rebuilding the golden set is what would close the gap, and that is an evaluation exercise rather than a technical one. Young women here also move between English, Swahili and Sheng inside a single sentence, and the agent is instructed to follow them into that register.

Authored corpus

Written for the audience and cited to WHO, UNICEF and Kenya MoH guidance.

The cost: it isn't a real-world ingestion problem. Source PDFs would have been messier, far above this reading level, and slower to get to a testable state.

09 Beyond the PoC what comes next

What this PoC leaves open.

Immediately
  • Verify every helpline number against a live source
  • Clinical review of the corpus by a health worker
  • Swahili golden set, so Swahili answers are measured rather than spot-checked
To measure properly
  • Retrieval scored over the golden set, using the chunk distances the API returns
  • A tighter distance threshold, and a multilingual embedding model for Swahili
  • Continuous evaluation over real transcripts
To reach her where she is
  • WhatsApp, SMS and voice — no smartphone or data assumed
  • Swahili corpus, to ground Swahili answers in Swahili source text; Sheng register
  • Testing with Youth Advisory Panels
Human handoff

A guardrail firing means the model has reached the edge of what it should do alone. That is when a person should take over.

  • Safeguarding disclosure → warm transfer to a counsellor, without making her repeat it
  • Twilio transfer; SendGrid case handoff with context
  • Write to a CRM; raise a follow-up task for the right team

transfer_to_number already exists on the agent, disabled only because there is no counsellor line to dial. Enabling it needs an operational commitment, not engineering.

To listen at scale

The same agent can run outbound — surveys and follow-ups that turn conversations into structured monitoring data. A research instrument, not just a helpline.

  • Structured procedures — a fixed sequence run identically every time; the right home for both a survey script and the safeguarding path
  • Redis — cache the few hundred questions asked thousands of times, invalidated on corpus version
  • QStash — queue outbound campaigns so volume never blocks a live conversation
