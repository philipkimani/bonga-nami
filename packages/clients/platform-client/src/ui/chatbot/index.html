01 Use case corpus, audience, success metrics

A girl asks a question she can't ask anyone else.

Adolescent girls in Kenya routinely miss school during their periods — through cost, lack of a private place to change, pain, or shame. The information gap is filled by myths. This assistant answers only from a curated corpus, in a register a 12-year-old trusts.

Audience
  • Girls aged 12–16, Kenya
  • Often shy, or asking "for a friend"
  • Carrying myths from home or online
  • For many, the first place they've asked
Corpus
  • Puberty: what changes, and when
  • Menstrual cycle, pain, management
  • Myths and the questions girls ask
  • 2,664 words · closed · cited
Success metrics
  • Grounded in corpus, not invented
  • Refuses cleanly when out of scope
  • Always routes disclosure of harm
  • Readable at age 11–12

02 Q&A flow what happens when she asks

The server mints a key, then gets out of the way.

One HTTPS request, then one long-lived socket. The platform signs a URL over HTTPS; everything after that — speech in, transcription, retrieval, the reply, audio out — streams over a single WebSocket held open for the conversation, because two-way audio cannot be done with request and response. The connection upgrades to WebRTC where the browser supports it.

03 Architecture frameworks and where state lives

What runs where — and what deliberately doesn't.

Orchestration runtime

Bonga answers questions. The same platform can also act on them — and that path already exists, at /webhooks/execute.

When a request needs more than an answer, the model doesn't call tools one at a time. It writes a short JavaScript program, and the runtime executes it against bound functions that reach the organisation's own systems, returning a single result.

Why that matters for tokens: every separate tool call is another round trip, and every round trip re-sends the entire conversation context to the model. Five tool calls means five times paying for the same context. One generated program collapses them into a single execution — fewer tokens, lower latency, and fewer places for the conversation to stall while a girl waits.

04 LLM & prompt engineering decisions and why

Every setting is an argument.

Generation
gemini-2.5-flash
Voice demands sub-second turns. A larger model buys reasoning this task doesn't need — the answer is in a 2,664-word corpus, not in the model's cleverness.
Temperature
0
Health answers shouldn't vary run to run, and the evaluation must be reproducible. Warmth comes from the prompt, not from sampling noise.
Prompt structure
7 sections
Personality, Environment, Tone, Goal, Boundaries, Safety, Certainty. Markdown headings are prioritised more reliably than prose paragraphs.
Persona
older sister
Not a clinician and not a peer. The register that gets a 13-year-old to ask a second question.
Corpus binding
explicit
Instructed to answer only from the knowledge base and to say so when it can't — then enforced independently at the platform layer, because a prompt alone can be talked around.
Anatomy language
correct terms
Euphemism teaches shame and makes disclosure of abuse harder. Explicitly instructed against slang for body parts.

05 Guardrails the audience-specific work

For a service used by children, the refusals are the product.

The platform's content filters terminate the call when they fire. Enabling the obvious ones — self-harm, sexual content — would hang up on a girl at the exact moment she needs help, or block the anatomy vocabulary the service exists to provide. They are off by deliberate choice.

Enforced · regenerates the answer
  • No diagnosis — describes what's common, never names her condition
  • No dosing — no drug names, doses or regimens
  • Nothing off-corpus — says so and refers on
  • Harm is routed — blocks any reply that fails to surface Childline 116
Also on
  • Focus — keeps the conversation in scope
  • Prompt-injection defence

The safeguarding rule is the inverse of a filter: it doesn't suppress the topic, it requires the right answer before the reply is allowed through.

06 Evaluation approach metrics and why these

Accuracy alone would miss the point.

A factually correct answer written at university reading level, or one that answers a disclosure of abuse as though it were a question about cramps, has failed this audience. So the metric set is deliberately split.

Groundedness

≥ 95%

Claims traceable to the corpus. The core accuracy measure.

Refusal accuracy

≥ 90%

Off-corpus probes declined rather than answered from general knowledge.

Safeguarding recall

100%

Disclosure scenarios that surface the helpline. Non-negotiable.

Reading level

≤ grade 6

Flesch-Kincaid. Audience-specific, deterministic, no judge needed.

Myth correction

≥ 90%

Myth-shaped prompts corrected rather than affirmed.

Method: a hand-built golden set from the corpus, plus off-corpus probes and safeguarding scenarios. Deterministic checks where possible; an LLM judge from a different model family than the generator, to avoid self-preference bias.

08 Trade-offs limitations and alternatives considered

What this costs.

Managed vs self-hosted retrieval

Chose the LLM API's built-in RAG: a working voice and text service in hours, no vector infrastructure.

The cost: retrieval is a black box. No precision/recall, no chunk-size ablation, no embedding comparison. The alternative — a pgvector pipeline — was considered and is partly scaffolded in the schema already.

Corpus scope

Narrowed from sexual and reproductive health to puberty and menstrual health alone.

The cost: real questions about contraception and consent get refused. The gain is a corpus that can actually be evaluated end-to-end, and a scope that is safe to operate live.

English-only corpus

Bonga speaks a wide range of languages, Swahili among them. The constraint here is mine, not the service's: one language means one golden set and directly comparable metrics.

The cost: girls in Kenya move between English, Swahili and Sheng. Extending is a corpus and evaluation exercise — translate the corpus, rebuild the golden set, re-run — not a technical barrier. Language detection is enabled today, so a Swahili question would be answered without a Swahili corpus to ground it.

Authored corpus

Written for the audience and cited to WHO, UNICEF and Kenya MoH guidance.

The cost: it isn't a real-world ingestion problem. Source PDFs would have been messier, far above this reading level, and slower to get to a testable state.

09 Beyond the PoC what comes next

What I'd do with more than a week.

Immediately
  • Verify every helpline number against a live source
  • Clinical review of the corpus by a health worker
  • Resolve the language-detection inconsistency
To measure properly
  • Self-hosted retrieval, for precision and recall
  • Chunk-size and embedding ablations
  • Continuous evaluation over real transcripts
To reach her where she is
  • WhatsApp, SMS and voice — no smartphone or data assumed
  • Swahili corpus and golden set; Sheng register
  • Testing with Youth Advisory Panels
To act, not just answer
  • Twilio warm transfer to a counsellor; SendGrid case handoff
  • Write to a CRM; raise a follow-up task for the right team
  • Book a clinic visit through a calendar integration
To listen at scale

The same agent can run outbound as well as inbound — calling or messaging beneficiaries to conduct surveys and follow-ups, and turning those conversations into structured monitoring data rather than a pile of transcripts. That is a research instrument, not just a helpline.

  • Structured procedures — a fixed sequence of typed steps run identically every time; the right home for both a survey script and the safeguarding path
  • Redis — cache the few hundred questions asked thousands of times, invalidated on corpus version so a corrected document never serves a stale answer
  • QStash — queue outbound campaigns so survey volume never blocks a live conversation
  • Pronunciation dictionaries — control how clinical terms and Swahili words are spoken
